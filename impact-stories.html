Improving access to clean water and helping protect children from dangerous water sources in Kamengo, Uganda.

Location: Kamengo, Mpigi District, Uganda Project period: March–April 2025 Implemented by: AGCF

A Community's Journey Toward Safer Water Access

Access to clean water is essential for the health, safety and wellbeing of children and families. When safe water sources are difficult to access, communities may depend on alternatives such as deep ponds and local wells. These locations can expose children to dangerous conditions and the risk of drowning.

Recognising the importance of safe water access, All God's Children Foundation (AGCF) implemented a borehole project in the Kamengo community, Mpigi District, Uganda. The project aimed to improve access to clean water while helping reduce children's exposure to hazardous water sources.

From Groundwork to Community Handover

The borehole project progressed through several stages during March and April 2025. Photographs document the work from the initial groundwork through to community handover.

  1. 6 March 2025 — Manual digging
    Initial groundwork began.
  2. 16 March 2025 — Drilling
    The project advanced to the drilling stage.
  3. 19 March 2025 — Pump installation
    The pump was installed as part of the water-point infrastructure.
  4. 22 March 2025 — Borehole top construction
    Work continued on the borehole's upper structure.
  5. 1 April 2025 — Community handover
    The project reached its community handover stage.

Improving Water Access and Children's Safety

The project was intended to provide the community with improved access to clean water and reduce the need for children to approach potentially dangerous water sources.

Deep ponds and local wells can pose serious drowning hazards, particularly when children approach them while collecting water or accompanying family members. Improving access to a borehole offers an alternative water source and supports wider efforts to protect children from preventable water-related dangers.

Safe water access and water-safety education are both important. Communities need accessible water sources, while children also need to understand water hazards and how to stay safe around them.

Why this matters: Improving access to a community water point can support daily needs while contributing to wider efforts to reduce children's exposure to dangerous water environments.

A Step Toward a Safer Community

For AGCF, this project represents more than the construction of a water point. It reflects a commitment to addressing community needs in ways that connect children's safety, family wellbeing and access to essential resources.

Sustaining these benefits requires responsible use, proper maintenance and attention to water quality. Continued community involvement is important to help protect the water point and support its long-term usefulness.

Looking Ahead

AGCF works toward communities where children can learn, grow and participate in everyday life with fewer preventable risks. The borehole project in Kamengo demonstrates how improving access to essential resources can form part of a wider commitment to child protection and community development.

By documenting this journey, AGCF aims to share its work transparently, recognise community participation and highlight the importance of practical, community-rooted solutions.
